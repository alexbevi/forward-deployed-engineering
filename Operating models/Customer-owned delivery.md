# Customer-owned delivery

## Mandate and mechanics

[[Articles/2026-09-14 - Arun Prasath E G - How an FDE engagement works]] and [[Articles/2026-09-14 - Arun Prasath E G - FDE engagements for owners]] describe written owner decisions, usable increments, and a named handover. [[Articles/2026-09-29 - Gartner - Vendor FDE abandonment forecast]] focuses on independence at transition. These sources align on ownership while differing in their definitions of the FDE role.

## Decision rights and ownership

[[Articles/2026-10-13 - OrNsoft team - How a forward-deployed engagement works|OrNsoft]] adds written phase gates, but supplies no demonstrated customer handover.

Distinguish business choices, engineering choices, approval for live changes, and continuing support. [[Articles/2026-09-21 - Pablo Bermejo - Turning intelligence into lasting value]] allows understood external dependencies; independence means the receiving organisation can make informed decisions and change the system without one person's memory.

[[Articles/2026-10-06 - CTERA - Forward deployed engineering for enterprise AI|CTERA]] proposes co-building and documenting from discovery, with completion when customer engineers can add the next use case without the vendor. This is a useful candidate demonstration of transfer, but the press release reports an intended model rather than a completed handover.

## Fit and failure modes

A candidate when the customer has an identified problem, practical access, a domain reviewer, and a capable receiving owner. A running prototype and a document alone do not establish independence. Unowned operations or inaccessible knowledge can make the engagement permanent by accident.

## Limits

[[Articles/2026-10-20 - Arushi Jain - Implementation to adoption handoff|Quivly's adoption handoff]] transfers account stewardship to customer success. It complements operational handover, but does not demonstrate the customer's ability to operate or change the system.

The APEG pages overlap and represent one commercial practice. Gartner's rates are forecasts, not measured outcomes. This model does not settle whether customer-specific delivery qualifies as FDE under a product-oriented definition.

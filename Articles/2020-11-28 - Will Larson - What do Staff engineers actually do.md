# What do Staff engineers actually do?

[Read the guide](https://staffeng.com/guides/what-do-staff-engineers-actually-do/).

## Summary

Larson argues that Staff engineers may continue to write software, build relationships, and coordinate projects, but those activities become supporting means rather than the definition of the role. Across different Staff archetypes, he proposes a shared foundation:

- Set and edit technical direction around organisational needs rather than personal technology preferences.
- Mentor engineers and, more importantly in his account, sponsor their opportunities and progress.
- Bring engineering context into unusual, time-sensitive organisational decisions while representing engineering broadly rather than a personal position.
- Explore ambiguous, consequential problems that incremental local optimisation cannot address.
- Perform necessary, often invisible glue work that helps important work continue and finish.

Technical direction is described as pragmatic advocacy for the company's technology over a long horizon. Larson compares part of the work to product management for technology: articulate a useful direction, align people around it, and make progress without treating every individual decision as decisive.

Exploration is presented as high-risk work for trusted engineers with broad skills. Examples include new business constraints, large infrastructure-cost reductions, multi-region strategy, and urgent capacity limits. This work requires enough organisational trust and resources for failure to be interpreted as information about the problem rather than automatic evidence against the engineer.

## Technical grounding

The practitioners quoted in the chapter describe different amounts and kinds of hands-on work: regular code contributions, small experiments, data analysis, code reading, and review. Larson's synthesis is that most Staff engineers write less code than earlier in their careers, sometimes none, while retaining technical contact that informs direction and decisions. Coding volume is therefore neither the definition of Staff work nor a substitute for grounded technical judgment.

He also argues that feedback cycles lengthen at Staff scope. Technical direction, organisational decisions, sponsorship, and exploration may take months or years to show results, making progress less immediately visible than the build-test-ship loop.

## Perspective and limits

This is a practitioner synthesis from a book chapter, illustrated by selected interview excerpts. The page does not provide a sampling method, organisational comparison, time-use data, or outcome evaluation. The quoted experiences enrich the account but do not constitute independent confirmation of Larson's framework.

The proposed common foundation should not be treated as a universal Staff job description. Career levels, archetypes, decision access, sponsorship expectations, coding responsibilities, and tolerance for exploratory failure vary by organisation. The claim that senior engineers should put business and organisational needs before personal technical interests also requires ethical, safety, professional, and long-term engineering constraints; current organisational preference is not automatically the correct decision.

## Staff synthesis

[Staff Engineer](../Staff%20Engineer.md) uses the guide to make several mechanisms explicit: technical direction connects engineering choices to organisational needs; sponsorship changes another engineer's opportunity rather than only advising them; Staff engineers contribute engineering context at decision points outside normal project routines; and hands-on work should ground judgment rather than serve as a code-volume target.

## FDE transfer

The guide is not about forward deployed engineering, but its enterprise-contract example exposes a direct intersection. A Staff-level FDE may be positioned to bring production, integration, support, and product constraints into a consequential customer or commercial decision before it hardens. The transfer requires representing broader engineering interests rather than one account or a personal technical preference, while leaving commercial and product authority with the named decision-makers.
